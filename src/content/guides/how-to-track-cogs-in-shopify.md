Cost of goods sold (COGS) is usually a store's largest cost, and the one most often wrong. A product without a cost looks fully profitable, which quietly inflates every profit number.

## Three ways to record COGS

1. **Shopify's "Cost per item"** on each product or variant. Simple, but it holds only the current cost.
2. **A spreadsheet** of costs by SKU. Flexible, but disconnected from your orders.
3. **A profit app** that reads Shopify's costs, accepts imports and keeps history.

## Costs change: use effective dates

Suppliers raise prices, freight costs move, and you switch manufacturers. If you overwrite the cost, your old months suddenly look less profitable than they were. Record each cost **with the date it took effect**, so every order uses the cost that applied when it was sold.

## Measure coverage

Ask: *what share of units sold in this period have a cost?* If the answer is 93%, your profit is overstated by the margin on the other 7%. Track coverage every month and fix the best-selling uncosted products first.

## Include landed costs

COGS should include what it took to get the product to your warehouse: duties, inbound freight and packaging you buy per unit. Leave outbound shipping to customers as a separate cost.
