Return on ad spend (ROAS) tells you how much revenue each dollar of advertising brought in. But revenue is not profit. **Break-even ROAS** tells you the ROAS you need before ads start making money.

## The formula

**Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ contribution margin before ad spend**

Contribution margin is what is left of each order after product, shipping, payment and other variable costs, divided by the order's revenue.

## Examples

| Contribution margin | Break-even ROAS |
|---|---|
| 60% | 1.67× |
| 48% | 2.08× |
| 40% | 2.50× |
| 25% | 4.00× |

A store with a 25% margin needs **4×** ROAS just to break even; a store with a 60% margin breaks even at **1.67×**. The same campaign can be profitable for one store and loss-making for another.

## What moves your break-even ROAS

- **Discounts** lower the margin and raise the bar.
- **Supplier price rises** do the same, often unnoticed.
- **Shipping and fee changes** add up across many orders.
- **Refunds** reduce the revenue each ad dollar really produced.

## ROAS, MER and POAS

- **ROAS** compares platform-attributed revenue with spend.
- **MER** (marketing efficiency ratio) compares *all* revenue with *all* ad spend.
- **POAS** (profit on ad spend) compares attributed **profit** with spend; above 1 means ads are profitable.
